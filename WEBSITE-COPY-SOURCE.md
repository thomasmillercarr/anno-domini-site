## 0. Voice rules (apply to every word on the site)

Non-negotiable. These are the clearest tell of whether the copy was written or generated.

- **British English.** Direct, restrained, editorial.
- **No exclamation marks anywhere.**
- **No dashes of any kind used as a pause** (em, en, or a hyphen standing in for a
  comma). Use commas or full stops instead. Hyphenated compound words are fine.
- No "hope this finds you well", no hype vocabulary, no growth-hacker gloss.
- Understated confidence. State the offer plainly and stop. "No catch, that's the
  whole reason."
- The catch is always stated, never hidden. Do not claim scarcity that is not true;
  capacity is "two at a time" because that is the arithmetic, not a sales tactic.

**Naming:** the site and decks use **Thomas**; the cold email signs **Tom**. Keep the
split. "A fractional AI partner" is a footer descriptor, not a headline.

## 2. Positioning — what we actually sell

**The one sentence:** Your diary, your CRM, your inbox and your spreadsheets already
do their jobs. Nothing makes them talk to each other, so the owner is the connective
tissue, by hand, every day. Anno Domini builds the system that does that work instead,
and you own it outright.

**The supporting frame:** the output of a full-time operations hire, without the hire.
A small firm cannot justify £30k a year for an ops person. £600 a month for the same
systems output is a different question entirely.

**The enemy named in the copy:** another subscription. Every owner we talk to is
already paying for tools that half work and do not connect. This is explicitly not one
more. No subscription, no seat licence, no lock-in. You own the system.

**Positioning against incumbents:** the tools you run (the CRM, the ops platform) are
software vendors. The build reads off their software instead of asking you to switch.
Integrate, never replace.

## 3. Problem section

Getting the work in, keeping it moving, staying visible, handling the admin: four jobs
a small firm runs by hand because nothing connects the tools that should do them.

The cost is quiet. Five hours a week on a database nobody works. Six hours on "any
news?" emails. Three hours every Friday rebuilding the same report. Evenings hunting
for the next client. None of it is the work you started the business to do, and none of

## 5. How it works

Every build runs the same way. One clear problem, one measurable output.

1. **Brief.** One call. We agree the single problem and the output that proves it
   worked. Scope stays tight and is written down.
2. **Build.** Three to seven days, heads down. A working system, built to be reused.
3. **Deliver.** A walkthrough, an SOP, a handover. You own it completely.
4. **Repeat.** Every delivery surfaces the next. The system grows with the business.

## 7. The offer ladder

You do not buy the menu. You pick one system, and there are three ways in.

### The Founding Build — free
One system from the menu, built free and yours to keep, the way we begin with people we
work with first. The terms are the offer, not the small print, and all of them are
stated up front:

- A 30-minute scoping call. Your system of record named, access granted live. If access
  or the data fails, the clock never starts.
- A one-page signed scope. It protects you from scope creep and binds you to nothing.
- **Live within 14 days** of access being granted.
- Handover: a walkthrough, a one-page SOP, and a question a week for 30 days.
- Running costs are your own accounts, typically under £30 a month, stated before build.
- **The stated catch:** a 20-minute debrief at the end and permission to write the build
  up as a case study, anonymised on request. No catch beyond that, that's the whole reason.
- **Capacity:** two at a time, one ever in active build. When both are taken, a dated
  waitlist, not silence.

### The Audit — £1,200, one-time
A discovery session, a written report ranking your top three opportunities by return,
and one working system built and **live within 10 days or you don't pay**. Includes the
walkthrough, SOP and 30 days of support. It pays for itself in the first build.

### The Retainer — £600 a month (founding rate)
Up to two build cycles a month, a weekly brief call, new tools as the business grows, a
quarterly review. **Founding pricing for the first clients only; new signings move to
£900+ after. Founding clients keep their rate.** Market context: comparable UK firms run
£997 to £2,500 a month.

Most start with one system on a real problem, and the rest of the menu opens on its own.

## 9. Trust — the three data rules

State these plainly on the site (and offer the one-page DPA before it is asked for):

1. **Your own accounts.** Everything runs in your accounts, not ours. Nothing to hold
   hostage; if we part ways, you keep the working system.
2. **Your data never trains anything.** It is used to run your systems, full stop.
3. **Human approval on anything that messages your customers.** Always, and stated, not
   hidden. Anything touching sensitive comms keeps a person in the loop.

Supporting: registered with the ICO, a data processing agreement offered up front,
professional indemnity insurance in force before any build starts.

## 10. FAQ (from the objection ledger)

Answers already agreed in `GTM-90-DAY-PLAN.md` §2.6. On-voice, ready to drop in.

- **What's the catch?** A 20-minute debrief and permission to write the build up. That's
  it. It's how the offer stays honest.
- **Why would anyone build for free?** To test whether the offer holds before pricing it.
  Two at a time is cheap market research, and it is the fastest way to prove the work.
- **Who pays to run it?** You do, in your own accounts, typically under £30 a month,
  stated before anything is built.
- **What if it breaks after 30 days?** A question a week during the window. After that,
  the retainer exists for exactly this.
- **Is my data safe?** Your accounts, a DPA up front, nothing trains on your data, human
  approval on anything outbound.
- **We already have software.** The build reads off what you have. It is the connective
  tissue between your tools, not a rival to them.
- **I'm not technical.** It is installed, documented and walked through. The SOP is one
  page.
- **What if you disappear?** You own everything and it runs in your accounts. Nothing is
  held hostage.
- **Why so cheap next to competitors?** Founding pricing, first clients only. It rises
  after the founding places are taken.
